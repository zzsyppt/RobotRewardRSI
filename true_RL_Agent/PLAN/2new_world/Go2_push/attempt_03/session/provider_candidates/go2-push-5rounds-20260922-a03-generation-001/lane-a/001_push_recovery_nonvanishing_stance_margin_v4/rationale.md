Canonical hypothesis: the anchor stance reward exp(-4*(tilt^2 + height_err^2)) flattens once a strong push drives the base away from nominal height, so a collapsed stance is nearly reward-free and the climb back is not reinforced, which matches the public failure evidence where height_collapse is the largest failure cluster (26 of 51 non-recoveries) and the high and repeated push strata recover least often (19/48 and 27/48). This candidate changes exactly one mechanism, the reward callable that shapes stance and height: the saturating exponential is replaced by a bounded piecewise quadratic-then-linear margin, quadratic inside a small deadband around nominal height and nominal tilt and strictly linear beyond it, with a steeper coefficient for height below nominal than above, so the gradient toward a nominal stance never vanishes and a deeper crouch is always scored worse than a shallower one. Every other term (stationary_v3, joint_pose, action_rate, fall), every weight and the rest of the component set are preserved from the anchor. Expected effect: a directed, non-saturating pull back to nominal height after the last pulse turns height-collapse endings into fresh stable dwells and lifts success mainly in the high and repeated push strata without disturbing the already-solid mild strata.
