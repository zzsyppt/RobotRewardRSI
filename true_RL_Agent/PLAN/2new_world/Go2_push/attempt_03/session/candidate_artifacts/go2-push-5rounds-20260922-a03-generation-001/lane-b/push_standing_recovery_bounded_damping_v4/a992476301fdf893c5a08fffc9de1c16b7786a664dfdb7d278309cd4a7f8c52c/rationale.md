Hypothesis. The closest prior program is the verified reference push_standing_recovery_stationary_reference_v3, which recovers almost every low and medium push but fails most high and repeated pushes as height collapse, side falls and backward falls. The suspected cause is its single stationarity callable, which keeps growing without bound once a push transient passes its clamp radius, so push-induced base velocity dominates credit assignment and the posture and termination mechanisms lose influence exactly during the recovery window. Exact code-level difference. That callable is replaced by bounded_damping, which keeps the reference small-signal costs, 0.5 times the square of scaled planar speed over 0.08 and 0.5 times the square of scaled angular speed over 0.5, but passes each of those costs through the rational saturation x divided by one plus x so both costs stay bounded by 1, and it relaxes the planar share by one minus 0.5 times the lean magnitude read from projected gravity so that a lean-triggered catch step is not charged the full damping cost. Every other mechanism and weight is preserved unchanged, including the upright_height posture reward, joint_pose, action_rate and the fall termination penalty. Prediction. Height collapse and residual falls should drop in the high and repeated push strata while low and medium stratum standing behaviour is retained because the small-signal cost geometry is unchanged.
