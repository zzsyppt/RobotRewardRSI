Closest prior program: push_standing_recovery_stationary_reference_v3, the current-best reference anchor. Sole mechanism change: the upright and height reward kernel; every other term, weight, sign, and the empty schedule and diagnostic lists are preserved. The anchor scored stance with a saturating Gaussian well over squared tilt and squared relative-height error at weight 3.0, so its shaping slope decays as the base sinks and a base held at 40-70 percent height loss still collected nearly the full 1.0-weighted stationary term. The observed height_collapse_dominated outcome is exactly that shallow optimum: staying low is cheap and the pull-up slope is too weak to escape. The replacement callable upright_recovery keeps weight 3.0 and returns 1.0 - (5*e + e*e + 2*u + 2*u*u), where e = clamp(abs(base_height_relative - 1) - 0.05, 0) and u = clamp(roll*roll + pitch*pitch - 0.01, 0), with clip_min -4.0 and clip_max 1.0. The height branch is linear-dominant, so unlike the anchor it never flattens: its slope stays about 5 per unit of relative height (about 15 per step at weight 3.0) at any depth, and a base at 30-50 percent relative height now earns roughly -6 to -10 per step instead of about 1, so any upward progress is strictly better than lying low at every height. The 0.05 height and 0.01 tilt-squared deadbands keep the near-goal value identical to the anchor, so the low and medium force strata that already recover keep the same signal, while the tilt branch becomes steeper than the anchor beyond about 30 degrees to also suppress the residual backward and side falls.
