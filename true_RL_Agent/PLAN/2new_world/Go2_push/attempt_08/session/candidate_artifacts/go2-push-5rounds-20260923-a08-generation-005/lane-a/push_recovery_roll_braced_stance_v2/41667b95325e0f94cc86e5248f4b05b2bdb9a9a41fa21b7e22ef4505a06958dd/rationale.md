Closest prior program: push_recovery_lateral_brace_v1, the accepted current best (public_dev mean 0.9115). Its dominant residual failure is fall_dominated with 10 fall_side and 7 not_recovered, and the high stratum is the weak point (37/48 vs 48/48 low and medium), so sideways toppling under strong pushes is the bottleneck. The sibling push_recovery_side_braced_stance_v1 (displacement-gated squared roll) was qualified_not_selected at 0.8385 with height_collapse_dominated, so gating the lateral term on displacement is negative evidence and is not repeated. Sole mechanism change here: a new RewardTerm roll_brace is added; upright_recovery, lateral_brace, stationary_v3, joint_pose, action_rate, fall, all their weights, signs, clips, and the empty schedule and diagnostic lists are preserved exactly. The anchor's lateral_brace is linear in |roll| beyond a 0.10 rad deadband, so its restoring slope is constant and weak once roll is large; a base tipping sideways under a strong push can accumulate large roll at only a linear cost while the height branch of upright_recovery dominates the gradient. roll_brace returns -clamp(|roll| - 0.10, 0, inf)^2, a quadratic lateral potential that is exactly zero inside the same 0.10 rad deadband and grows with the square of roll excess, so it adds restoring slope that increases with tilt magnitude exactly in the large-roll regime where the anchor's linear term is too weak. It is asymmetric by construction: it never penalizes pitch or forward/backward motion, so it cannot suppress the sagittal recovery transient. The clip [-2, 0] with weight -1.0 bounds the weighted contribution, and the shared deadband keeps the nominal stance value identical to the anchor. Success definition, push schedule, and all runner-owned surfaces are untouched.
