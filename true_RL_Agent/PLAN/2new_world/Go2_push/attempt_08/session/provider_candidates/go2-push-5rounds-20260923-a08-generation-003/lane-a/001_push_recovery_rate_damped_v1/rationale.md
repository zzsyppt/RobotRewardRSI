Closest prior program: push_recovery_nonsaturating_stance_v1, the accepted current best (public_dev mean 0.8802; outcome counts success_recovered 169, fall_side 15, not_recovered 4, height_collapse 3, fall_forward 1; strata low 47/48, medium 43/48, high 37/48, repeated 42/48). Sole mechanism change: one added RewardTerm, fall_rate_damping, weight -1.0, clip [0.0, 8.0]; every existing callable, weight, sign, clip and the empty diagnostics and schedules lists are preserved exactly. The anchor prices posture (relative base height and tilt, plus the deadbands, in upright_recovery) and world-frame speed magnitude (stationary_v3), so vertical base velocity never enters the program at all, and the only angular-rate channel is the yaw-mixing world-frame magnitude inside stationary_v3, whose effective 0.25 factor prices a 2.5 rad/s tipping rate at 0.125 of output, which is nothing next to the -20 terminal fall term. The transient that actually produces the residual side, forward and low-height failures, the base dropping and rotating faster than the legs can replace support, therefore carries no dense price before termination. fall_rate_damping adds that missing channel on already-public inputs: sink = clamp(0.05 - base_lin_vel_scaled[:,2], min=0) prices downward world-z velocity beyond about 0.1 m/s, and tip = clamp(norm(base_ang_vel_scaled[:,:2]) / 0.25 - 1.0, min=0) prices the horizontal roll and pitch tipping rate beyond about 1.25 rad/s while deliberately leaving yaw unpriced, because spinning in place is not a fall. The cost sink + sink^2 + tip + tip^2 is identically zero in the near-goal stance where the low and medium strata already recover, grows smoothly with both the depth and the rate of the deviation, and its 8.0 cap is an outlier bound that does not bind in the operating range. Both channels are rate-gated rather than posture-gated, so a base that is displaced or tilted but slow keeps exactly the anchor signal; that is what distinguishes this from the generation-two height-times-tilt coupling and linear tilt ramp, which raised the price of being displaced at all and cost the high and repeated strata their recovery margin, and from the round-one gated planar damping, which only rescaled the existing planar term.
